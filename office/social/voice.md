# CHOSN on X: the voice guide

For the founder to read and say yes or no. Written 2026-10-22 by the Social team.

## The voice in one line
We are the critic. We say what the shoe is made of, how it is built, where it is made, and what it costs in rupees against what you get. We praise what is good, we question what is marketing, and we never go after a person.

## Do
- Lead with the material or the construction, then the rupee price. One fact the reader did not have.
- Put a number in: rupees, grams, a percentage, a style code.
- Say "my take" in the text whenever it is an opinion. Facts and opinion never share a sentence without that marker.
- Name the source family in the post when it matters ("Nike's own spec", "one review", "our store scrape"), and keep the link in the draft file so the founder can add it as a reply.
- Say what you do not know ("origin not confirmed"). Admitting a gap earns more trust than a guess.
- Compare with a better or cheaper alternative when we can source both.
- End roughly every second post with a real question.
- Say the style code. The same name can be made of different materials.

## Do not
- No hype slang: cope, dope, heat, fire, grail, bussin, drip, insane, crazy, goated, steal.
- No "fake" or "scam" about any product or company without proof. We say "unverified" or "the listing does not show X".
- No insults, no pile-ons, no names of private people. We criticise products, prices and marketing.
- No copying publisher text. Headline and credit only.
- No claim without a source or the "my take" marker. If a claim cannot be supported, cut it.
- No posting links to stores we cannot vouch for. No price promises: prices are "listed at", with the date.
- No emoji walls. At most one, only if it adds meaning.

## Formats (rotate; never the same take within 30 days)
1. Teardown: what each part is made of and how it is joined.
2. Price reality check: rupee price against parts and alternatives.
3. Retail versus resale: what you pay and why.
4. What you are paying for: the technology name versus the actual part.
5. Myth check: a common claim, tested against sources.
6. Real versus fake basics: how to check, with no accusations against any seller.
7. Collab spotlight: what a collab changes in the shoe, and what it does not.
8. Community question: one clear question people can answer in a line.
9. Sale: a listing at a date, store price against the MRP the store shows. Never "steal" or "must buy"; say what to check (style code, sizes, returns).
10. New releases and collabs: the release, the price in rupees, how firm the date is, and what a collab changes in the shoe beyond the logo.
11. Unexpected news: headline and credit only, then a question or our take marked as an opinion.
12. Random: culture and retail prompts with a point of view (`random.md`).

## Ten rewrites we never post (hype)
1. "Dunk Lows are straight heat, you need these in your rotation." Nothing checkable, no price, no material.
2. "Air Force 1s never miss. Timeless. Cop and thank me later." A slogan, not a fact.
3. "Jordan 1 Lows are fire, easy grail." Hype slang, and it hides that the spec varies by style code.
4. "Yeezy 350s are the ultimate comfort, Boost is insane." Unsourced superlative.
5. "Sambas are the dopest shoe of the year, don't sleep." Slang, pressure, no price.
6. "Campus 00s are an absolute steal at any price." Contradicts every price check we do.
7. "Superstars are pure leather, period." States as fact what the brand's own page contradicts.
8. "NB 550 is the best shoe under ₹15k, nothing else comes close." "Best" with no comparison.
9. "Speedcats are goated, wear them everywhere." Ignores the lab test's own downsides.
10. "Gel-1130 is a scam at this price, avoid." Calls a product a scam with no proof; we say what the parts are and give the price instead.

## Reply tone
Short, specific, and polite. If someone corrects us, thank them, check the source, and correct the fact bank. If someone disagrees with an opinion, that is fine; answer with the fact, not a fight. We do not argue with anyone who is being rude.
