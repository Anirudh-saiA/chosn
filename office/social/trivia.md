# Interesting facts bank

Used by `scripts/social_posts.py` for the "fact" slot. Every fact has a source link; where sources disagree the text says so. Checked 2026-10-23 from search results; pages were not opened in full, so skim a source before posting. Format: `@item <id> | <confidence>`, then `text:` and `sources:`. Not reused within 30 days.

@item af1-origin | Reported
text: Air Force 1 fact: designer Bruce Kilgore took inspiration from a Nike hiking boot called the Approach. It launched in October 1982, and Nike claimed it cushioned shock 30 percent better than a standard shoe. Kilgore called it his first basketball shoe.
sources: https://www.nike.com/a/air-force-1-history

@item samba-origin | Reported
text: Samba fact: sources say Adi Dassler designed it in 1949 to let football players train on icy, hard ground. It debuted at the 1950 World Cup, and skaters adopted it in the 1990s. Sources differ on 1949 versus 1950. Today it is a fashion shoe.
sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas_Samba ; https://www.thegoodlifespace.com/blogs/features/the-adidas-samba-a-brief-history-of-the-iconic-sneaker

@item superstar-shelltoe | Reported
text: Superstar fact: adidas calls it the first low-top leather basketball shoe with serious protection, thanks to the rubber shell toe. The design dates to 1969, the debut to 1970, and adidas credits its France team with the ribbed toe. The shell toe was a safety feature first.
sources: https://www.adidas.com/us/blog/469118-adidas-superstar-shoes-a-history-of-shelltoe-style ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas_Superstar

@item dunk-college | Reported
text: Dunk fact: it launched in 1985 as a college basketball shoe, part of a "Be True To Your School" campaign in team colours. Skaters adopted it, and in 2002 Nike made the SB Dunk with extra padding and Zoom Air. Sources differ on the exact list of schools.
sources: https://www.shelflife.co.za/magazine/factsfriday-the-nike-dunk ; https://designscene.net/2024/05/nike-dunk.html
