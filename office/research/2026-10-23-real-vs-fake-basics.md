# Real versus fake basics for Nike, adidas, Puma, ASICS and New Balance (for the Social posts and the legit check)
**Answer / recommendation:** The honest finding is that **the brands publish very little**. Only New Balance has an official "counterfeit" page with a real check (a QR code on the tongue label); ASICS and Puma publish where to buy and what warranty excludes; Nike and adidas publish no consumer guide I could find. So the Social posts should teach **five checks that hold for every brand and that brands or authenticators support**, and avoid brand-specific "tells" that spread from blogs and go out of date. Posts must never say a particular seller or store is fake or real. The planned legit check should be built as a **checklist plus a "compare with the brand's own listing" step**, not as a verdict.
**Confidence:** medium. **Why:** brand statements were read from snippets or pages in places (New Balance pages returned 403 to my tools), authenticator guides did not surface in search, and almost all other advice online is from content-farm blogs, which I excluded.

**Labels:** Confirmed = the brand's own page; Reported = authenticator or reputable outlet; Unconfirmed = blogs or could not verify.

## Five checks that work for all brands
| Check | What to say in a post | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Where it was bought** | Genuine product comes from the brand's own stores and its authorised retailers. ASICS, Puma India and New Balance say so; Nike's returns rules require an authorised dealer. | Confirmed | [ASICS India terms](https://www.asics.co.in/termsconditions), [Puma India warranty](https://in.puma.com/in/en/help/warranty-claims), [New Balance](https://www.newbalance.com/counterfeit_landing_page.html), [Nike guidelines](https://www.nike.com/help/claims/guidelines) |
| **2. A price far below MRP** | "Too good to be true" is a red flag. | Confirmed (New Balance, ASICS) | same pages; [ASICS beware of scams (Singapore)](https://www.asics.com/sg/en-sg/customer-service/beware-of-scams/beware-of-scams.html) |
| **3. Look-alike websites** | A brand name in a web address does not mean a licensed site (brand name plus "shop" or "outlet"). Not a certain sign. | Confirmed (New Balance, ASICS) | same pages |
| **4. Compare with the brand's own listing** | Find the brand's India page for the same style code and compare colours, materials and details. The style code is a clue only: codes are easy to copy. | Reported (authenticators and guides); not endorsed by a brand | see "Disputed" below |
| **5. No single sign proves it** | A tag, QR scan or "authenticated" sticker is not proof by itself. | Reported | [Complex on Nike v. StockX](https://www.complex.com/sneakers/a/victor-deng/nike-stockx-lawsuit-counterfeit-sneakers) |

## By brand
**New Balance** (the only brand with an official check)
- Tongue-label data matrix is being replaced by a **QR code from May 2025**. Scan it and compare the size and details on the page with the shoe; a mismatch means not authentic. Confirmed (snippet of the official page; page date not shown). [newbalance.com counterfeit page](https://www.newbalance.com/counterfeit_landing_page.html)
- New Balance says it does not sell through individuals, street vendors, flea markets or house parties, and that it cannot authenticate products (report fakes to counterfeits@newbalance.com). Confirmed.
- Model-specific tells (misspelt "durance" on a 1906U outsole, a glossy tongue print) come from a resale marketplace with a commercial interest. Reported only. [SNKRDUNK](https://snkrdunk.com/en/magazine/?p=500849)
- Caution: older pairs have the data matrix, not the QR; a printed QR can be copied, so compare what the page shows.

**ASICS**
- No official "spot a fake shoe" list. ASICS India says authentic product is sold only on asics.co.in, authorised stores and authorised marketplaces, and warns about unauthorised social-media sellers. Counterfeit or unauthorised-channel products get no warranty. Confirmed (found by search; not all pages fetched). [terms](https://www.asics.co.in/termsconditions), [warranty](https://www.asics.co.in/warranty-policy)
- ASICS' regional "Beware of Scams" pages list fake-site signs (brand plus "shop" or "outlet" in the web address, very low prices) and say these are not definitive. Confirmed. [Singapore page](https://www.asics.com/sg/en-sg/customer-service/beware-of-scams/beware-of-scams.html)
- ASICS' corporate page says fake quality has "improved significantly". Confirmed. [corp.asics.com](https://corp.asics.com/en/focus/ip/ac)
- Logo, glue and foam-shine tips: Unconfirmed (blogs).

**Puma**
- No official consumer guide. Puma India's warranty page excludes counterfeit or non-genuine products and products from unauthorised sellers, and lists its own site, stores and authorised marketplaces. Confirmed. [help page](https://in.puma.com/in/en/help/warranty-claims)
- Style-number and box-label checks: Unconfirmed (blogs). The claim of a numbered anti-counterfeit label from a German headquarters comes from a 2010 blog; treat it as outdated.

**Nike (including Jordan)**
- No official consumer guide found. Nike's returns guidelines bar counterfeits and require purchase from an authorised dealer. Confirmed (snippet). [guidelines](https://www.nike.com/help/claims/guidelines)
- One authenticator-style article lists twelve tells for one model (Air Jordan 4 "Cement": box-label font, speckle density, heel tab, tongue height). It is from **2016** and says fakes have improved. Reported, model-specific, dated. [Complex](https://www.complex.com/sneakers/a/brandon-richard/air-jordan-iv-4-cement-real-fake-legit-check)
- Useful fact for the legit check: Nike's India page lists a **country of origin per style code** (for example Dunk Low HF5441-105: Vietnam; Air Jordan 1 Low DC0774-160: Indonesia). A pair whose tag disagrees with the brand listing for that style code is worth a closer look. This is my inference from Confirmed data (see `2026-10-23-india-mrp-and-specs.md`); no brand states it as a test.

**adidas**
- No official consumer guide or counterfeit-reporting page found. adidas' app privacy page mentions a label-scanning feature that checks if a product is genuine; I could not confirm how or where it works. Confirmed that it is mentioned, details Unconfirmed. [privacy policy](https://adidasapp.adidas.com/legal/en-GB/privacy_policy.html)
- Claims about a 12-digit inner code, laser-etched numbers, "ICEK" lookups and Boost texture come from blogs and conflict. Unconfirmed. A failed lookup is not proof of a fake.

## Disputed or outdated claims to avoid in posts
- **"Made in" country as a tell.** No reliable source supports it; genuine pairs are made in several countries and even one style code can list several (Air Force 1 CW2288-111: China, India, Vietnam).
- **UV or black-light test.** Presented as reliable by resale guides; two sources call it optional or material-dependent. Treat as weak.
- **QR, NFC, barcode or serial checks.** Fakers can copy printed codes. Only the New Balance method (scan, then compare the page's details to the shoe) is brand-confirmed.
- **Authenticator accuracy claims** ("99.1%") are marketing, not independent. A court case reported by Complex found a resale marketplace liable for counterfeit Nike pairs it had authenticated, which later settled. Reported.
- **Model-specific box-label and stitching tells** age quickly (2016 article above).
- Search results for "how to spot fake <brand>" were dominated by content-farm and SEO pages; none were used.

## What this means for the Social posts
- Safe to post: the five checks above, with the brand sources. "Where it was bought" and "price far below MRP" are Confirmed.
- Not safe: any line naming a seller, store or marketplace as fake or real; any model-specific tell without a date and source; "if the UV light shows X it is fake".
- Frame: "Here is what the brand itself says" first, then "common checks (not proof)".

## Risks and unknowns
- New Balance's page was read through search snippets; the live page may say more. Nike, adidas and Puma may have guides I could not reach (their pages returned 403 to automated tools).
- GOAT, eBay Authenticity Guarantee, Highsnobiety and Sneaker Con guides did not appear in my searches; a deeper pass on those could add Reported sources.
- No India government guidance specific to footwear was found; FICCI CASCADE covers counterfeits in general. [EU IP helpdesk summary, April 2026](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/india-highlights-growing-threat-counterfeit-goods-and-expiry-tampering-2026-04-11_en) (Reported).
- Not legal advice.

## What I would do next
1. Social: build the posts from the five checks; keep every brand-specific line out until sourced.
2. Founder or someone with a normal browser: open the New Balance counterfeit page and the Nike and adidas help centres and confirm there is no consumer guide.
3. R&D follow-up: a deeper pass on GOAT, eBay Authenticity Guarantee and Entrupy for public checklists, with dates.
